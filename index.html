Every line on the tennis court has a name and a job. The court is like a map. Once you know the map, the game makes sense.

The lines and what they mean

Baseline The back line. You stand behind it to serve. Hit balls deep to here to push your opponent back."I serve from here."
Service line The line in front of the net. Your serve must land between this line and the net."Serve must land before this."
Center service line Splits the service area into two boxes — deuce side (right) and ad side (left)."Splits the boxes."
Singles sideline The inner side line. In singles, balls outside this line are OUT."My singles wall."
Doubles sideline The outer side line. In doubles, balls outside this line are OUT."My doubles wall."
Doubles alley The strip between the two sidelines. Only used in doubles play."Only for doubles."
Service boxes Four boxes near the net. Your serve must bounce inside the diagonal one."Where my serve must land."
Center mark The little tick on the baseline. Tells the server where the middle is."Middle of the baseline."
No man's land The space between the service line and baseline. Try not to stand here too long during a rally."Don't camp here."
The net Center is 3 feet tall (lower for red ball: 2'9"). Touching the net = lose the point."Net is lava."

Tennis grows with you

Tennis has stages. Each stage teaches different things. You start with one color of ball and grow into the next. There is no rush. Every great player started where you are.

🔴

Red Stage

What you learn here: how to make friends with the ball, how to swing, how to rally, how the court works. The ball is slow on purpose, so your body has time to learn the moves. This is where every shot becomes yours.

"Make friends with the ball."
🟠

Orange Stage

What you learn here: how to play real points, how to use the whole court, how to add spin, and how to think one shot ahead. The ball moves a little faster — so now your brain starts playing tennis too.

"Now I think and hit."
🟢

Green Stage

What you learn here: how to play full matches, how to read your opponent, how to build a point, and how to handle the pressure of close scores. This is where strategy shows up.

"Play smart, not just hard."
🎾

Yellow Stage

What you learn here: the full game, the way pros play. Bigger courts, faster balls, longer matches. By now your strokes, your rules, and your spirit are all yours. You're a tennis player for life.

"My game. My rules. Forever."

Tennis for Kids: Basic Rules

Animated kid-friendly explanation of the court and how the game is played.

Tennis has a few rules and a special way of counting points. Once you know them, every game makes sense. Read one rule. Look at the picture. Watch the video. Then play.
👤

1. Who plays

Singles: 1 player vs 1 player. Doubles: 2 vs 2. You are on opposite sides of the net.

"One vs. one. Or two vs. two." ▶ Tennis basics for kids
🪙

2. Who serves first

Spin a racquet or flip a coin. The winner picks: serve, receive, or which side. The other player gets the leftover choice.

"Spin the racquet." ▶ How tennis is played
🎾

3. The serve goes diagonal

Stand behind the baseline. Hit the ball over the net. It MUST bounce in the service box on the OPPOSITE diagonal side.

"Cross the court." ▶ Beginner serve guide
2️⃣

4. Two chances to serve

If your first serve misses, you get a second try. If both miss, that's a "double fault" and your opponent wins the point.

"Two tries each turn." ▶ Beginner serve guide
🎯

5. The ball must land in

If the ball lands inside the lines (or ON the line) it's IN. Outside the line = OUT. The server's lines are just the service box. After the serve, you use the whole court.

"On the line is in." ▶ Rules of tennis
🟡

6. Bounce once, then hit

Let the ball bounce ONE time before you hit it (or hit it before it bounces — that's a volley). If it bounces twice, your opponent wins the point.

"One bounce. My turn." ▶ Tennis basics for kids
🚫

7. Don't touch the net

If you, your racquet, or your clothes touch the net while the ball is in play, you lose the point.

"Net is lava." ▶ Rules of tennis
🔢

8. The funny way to count

0 = "love" · 1st point = 15 · 2nd = 30 · 3rd = 40 · 4th = game. You must win by 2. Tied at 40-40 is called "deuce."

"Love. Fifteen. Thirty. Forty. Game." ▶ Scoring explained
🏆

9. Games into sets

Win 6 games (with a 2-game lead) = win a set. Most matches are best-of-3 sets. Red ball matches are usually shorter — first to 7 points wins.

"Win by two." ▶ Sets & matches
🤝

10. Be a great sport

Call your own lines fair. If you're not sure, give it to your opponent. After the match, walk to the net and shake hands. Tennis is a game of respect.

"Shake. Smile. Say good game." ▶ Pro sportsmanship
Love, fifteen, thirty, forty, game. The score in tennis is its own little song.

Every shot in tennis has a name, a grip, and a swing path. You already know how to "bounce and hit." Now let's name the strokes and learn the secrets.

The 5 main strokes

➡️

Forehand

The shot you hit on your strong side (right side if you're right-handed). Grip: Eastern — like shaking hands with the racquet.

"Low to high. Finish high." ▶ Forehand video
⬅️

Backhand

The shot on your other side. Most kids start with TWO hands on the racquet for power.

"Turn. Swing. Finish high." ▶ 2-hand backhand
🚀

Serve

The shot that starts every point. Toss the ball up, hit it down into the service box. Beginners can serve UNDERHAND from the baseline.

"Toss. Reach. Hit." ▶ Serving basics
🛡️

Volley

A shot you hit BEFORE the ball bounces. Used near the net. Short punch, no big swing.

"Block like a wall." ▶ Volley basics
⬆️

Overhead (Smash)

When the ball is high over your head, you reach up and smash it down. Like a serve in mid-rally.

"Point. Then crush." ▶ Overhead intro
🪁

Lob

A high, soft shot that floats over your opponent's head. Used when they come close to the net.

"Scoop low. Lift HIGH." ▶ Lob basics

Spin: the secret of every great shot

🌀

Topspin

Brush UP on the ball. The ball arcs over the net and dives down into the court. Lets you swing harder while still keeping it in.

"Brush UP." ▶ How spin works
🍂

Slice (Backspin)

Brush DOWN on the ball. The ball floats and stays low after the bounce. Great for surprising your opponent.

"Brush DOWN." ▶ Slice basics
➖

Flat

No spin. The ball flies straight. Hit through the back of the ball, level. The simplest shot to start with.

"Hit straight through." ▶ Basic forehand

The grips (how to hold the racquet)

🤝

Eastern (Forehand)

Shake hands with the racquet. Best for beginners. Easy and forgiving.

"Shake hands." ▶ Forehand & backhand grips
🪓

Continental (Hammer)

Hold the racquet like you're holding a hammer. Used for serves, volleys, and overheads.

"Hold the hammer." ▶ Continental grip
✋✋

Two-Handed Backhand

Bottom hand: continental. Top hand: like a forehand grip. Hands touching, no gap. Most kids' favorite backhand.

"Both hands together." ▶ 2-hand backhand

The Ready Position

Before every shot, get ready:

  • Feet wider than shoulders, knees a little bent.
  • Weight on the balls of your feet — like a goalie.
  • Racquet held in front, both hands on it.
  • Eyes WATCHING the ball.
"Athletic. Eyes up. Ready." ▶ Ready position & split step

The Five Steps of Every Shot

1️⃣

See it

Watch the ball off your opponent's racquet. Is it coming to your forehand or backhand?

"Watch the ball." ▶ Tennis basics for kids
2️⃣

Move

Get your feet to the right spot BEFORE you swing. Small fast steps.

"Feet first." ▶ Footwork in 10 min
3️⃣

Turn

Turn your shoulders sideways. Take the racquet back early.

"Shoulders sideways." ▶ Forehand technique
4️⃣

Hit

Swing low to high. Contact ball in front of your body. Watch it leave the strings.

"Contact in front." ▶ Stroke contact point
5️⃣

Finish

Follow through over your opposite shoulder. Then go back to ready position.

"Follow through." ▶ Shadow swing & finish
The structure stays the same so Alexander can predict what's coming. The content inside the structure changes so it never gets boring. Four lesson formats roll in over the year — one at a time, never all at once. Each format scales to a 60-min lesson with Jordan or a 30-min practice with Daddy.

Why formats, not single-skill weeks

Alexander needs predictable structure. He also needs novelty — but novelty inside the structure, not novelty AS the structure. So instead of "this week we do uppies, next week we do dribbles," every practice follows the same shape: warm up → play → new skill → play with skill → drill → clean up. The shape is the constant. What goes inside each block is what evolves.

Built-in transitions (water cooler, ball machine adjustments, lights on/off) are part of the practice, not interruptions. They reset attention between blocks. Plan for them.

The Four Formats

One format starts. A second is added at week 7. A third at week 27. A fourth at week 40. After each addition, formats rotate — never converge.

Fruit Salad NICOLAS' FAVORITE

Each player picks a fruit (banana, apple, grape, orange). Stand at cones near the net. Daddy calls out a fruit and tosses a ball — that fruit's player runs in and hits a volley. When Daddy yells "FRUIT SALAD!" — everyone drops their racquets and races to the back fence and back to their cone! Last one back does a silly dance. First one back picks the next fruit.

Why it's perfect for the brothers: built-in regulation breaks (the running interludes), social and silly, predictable structure with novelty, and a sibling can lead "the call" — Nicolas calling, Alexander hitting, then switch.

Hungry Crocodile

Daddy calls "Alexander!" and feeds a volley. Miss = lose a limb. First arm goes (hit lefty), then a leg (hop), then both legs (kneel). Last to survive wins. Hilarious.

Red Light, Green Light Tennis

Daddy says "green light" — you rally normally. "Red light" — both freeze with racquets up. "Yellow light" — slow motion.

Cone Pyramid

5 cones on opposite side, far away from each other. Each cone you hit with a forehand = 1 point. First to all 5 wins.

King of the Court

Make any drill more fun

  • Add a story. "The forehand is a lion's roar — low to high, big finish."
  • Add silly cues. "Pet the dog, then catch the bird." (Low racquet, then high finish.)
  • Add a target. A cone, a hoop, a chalk square. Aim is more fun than just hitting.
  • Add a count-out-loud. "1!... 2!... 3!" Builds rhythm. Builds confidence.
  • Add a victory move. Fist pump, dance, secret handshake after every great shot.
  • Add music. Bring a speaker. Pick the songs. Tennis to a beat.
